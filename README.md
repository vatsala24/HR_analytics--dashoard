HR Analytics — Employee Attrition Analysis

Project Overview

This project analyzes employee attrition patterns using the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset. Microsoft Excel was used for data cleaning, exploratory analysis, PivotTables, PivotCharts, KPI calculations and interactive dashboard development.

Dataset Overview

- Total employee records: 1,470
- Employees who left: 237
- Active employees: 1,233
- Overall attrition rate: 16.12%

Key Findings

1. Department: Sales has the highest attrition rate at 20.63%.
2. Age: Employees under 30 have the highest attrition rate at 27.91%.
3. Income: The Low Income category has an attrition rate of 21.76%.
4. Overtime: Employees working overtime have an attrition rate of 30.53%, compared with 10.44% for employees not working overtime.
5. Job Satisfaction: Employees with low satisfaction have a higher attrition rate (19.68%) than those with high satisfaction (11.33%).
6. Tenure: Employees with 0–2 years of service have the highest attrition rate at 29.82%.

Business Recommendations

- Review compensation competitiveness and salary progression.
- Investigate overtime practices and workload distribution.
- Strengthen onboarding and employee retention initiatives.
- Improve employee engagement and satisfaction.
- Review career development and promotion opportunities.

Conclusion

The analysis identifies several employee groups with comparatively higher attrition rates. Overtime, shorter organizational tenure and lower income categories are important areas for further investigation.

The interactive Excel dashboard enables HR stakeholders to explore employee attrition patterns using department, gender and overtime filters.

The findings represent associations in the available dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of the underlying causes of employee attrition.
